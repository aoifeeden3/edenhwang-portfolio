Design notes
Note on the design

This one comes from Animal Crossing, the game I played as a child and still open when I want somewhere quiet to be. None of it is copied. What I took was the grammar: a world with no sharp corners anywhere, where every object is rounded, outlined in a warm brown rather than black, and slightly too chunky for its own size.

The ground is one flat green printed with little tufts. Signs are wooden boards with rounded ends, hung so they tilt. Everything you can interact with looks placed rather than built, which is why the cards here sit on the lawn with a shadow under them instead of being ruled into a grid.

The palette, and how it moves

Two big calm fields, sky blue above and grass green below, with cream standing in for white on every surface that holds text. Against that, three small pops used sparingly: tulip red for anything that acts, tulip yellow for a reward, blossom pink for decoration. Wood brown does the work black usually does. The restraint is the point: in that game the colour is loud but the quantity is small, so the eye rests.

Four moves. Petals fall across the header on their own timing and rotate as they go. Clouds drift from one side to the other, slowly enough that you notice them only if you stop. Cards hop up and tilt a degree when you hover, then settle, like an item being set down. The wooden section signs sway on their posts, all the time, at slightly different moments. And the tree at the edge of the lawn can be shaken: apples drop and scatter on the grass, and one time in four one of them is golden.
